tl;dr Smart contract developer decides to jump headfirst into ricing with
agents.

I'd heard that Hyprland might take care of some GPU crashing that I was
experiencing, and took the plunge. Agents definitely make Linux troubleshooting
a world easier! Once I was at it, I decided to take things farther. Ricing had
been on my periphery for years, and with the power just a few subscriptions
away, I felt like it was time.

Doing some basic ricing on my desktop made me turn my attention to an old
laptop. The most recent thing I'd done with it was brick the Debian installation
on it, so I decided it was time for a very drastic redesign. Repartitioning away
from an old Windows partition that was keeping the Linux boot space unreasonably
small, it now sports niri on top of NixOS. I started by crafting a comprehensive
plan that we've been following, making amendments along the way. After all, no
product spec survives contact with implementation.

There's a slide deck linked above to give a better idea of what we've done and
what we're working on. The deck itself is my first foray with
[Impeccable](https://impeccable.design), meaning the slide deck itself is also
demonstrating the same spirit of exploration and experimentation that the rest
of the project is.
